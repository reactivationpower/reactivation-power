# Decompression Script Audit

## Baseline

- Lint result: 126 errors and 14 warnings.
- Word count: 3,982 total.
- Step text and titles: 3,616 words.
- Button labels: 366 words.

## Long multi-option questions

- `opening_question` combined three possible answers into one 30-word question.
- All three `digging_in*` screens stacked examples and follow-up questions.
- `making_it_real` repeatedly offered long lists of time and frequency options.
- `bridge_no` asked the same point three ways.
- `obj_think` combined its question with three answer options.

## Flourishes and intensifiers

- `transition` and `transition_fully_back` used “absolutely.”
- `making_it_real` and `obj_think` used “honestly.”
- `lapsed_commitment` used “thrilled.”
- Several screens used dramatic language about “holding its ground,” warning signals, and being “full of energy.”

## Sales stock phrases

- `recommendation` used “honored” and the time-or-money closer.
- `making_it_real` used the “every single day” stock line.
- `lapsed_felt_fine` used “Here’s the funny thing.”
- `lapsed_wait_pain` used the banned “ahead of it instead of behind it” line.
- Objection screens relied on rehearsed “that’s exactly why” transitions.

## Invented patient quotes and memories

- `resp_coming_back` and `resp_fully_back` claimed, “I remember how much relief you had.”
- `lapsed_commitment` claimed to remember the patient’s original goal and what they told the office.
- `resp_doing_great` prompted the patient to remember how bad things were.
- `making_it_real` supplied scripted patient reactions and imagined “best” and “worst” days.

## Token pile-ups

- `recommendation` repeated legacy `{{rec_pronoun}}` forms throughout one paragraph.
- `making_it_real`, `obj_came_back_stopped`, `obj_fixed_for_good`, `obj_injections_surgery`, `obj_managing_own`, `lapsed_cost`, `lapsed_moved`, `obj_think`, and `obj_send_info` used legacy `{{rec_*}}` tokens.
- No screen needed repeated `{{provider_name}}`; the rewrite uses it once on `recommendation`.

## Unparenthesized coaching directions

- `resp_coming_back` and `resp_fully_back` left caller directions as spoken text.
- Every niche objection ended with an unparenthesized “Confirm and continue” direction.
- `making_it_real` mixed coaching, sample reactions, and spoken lines inside the same paragraphs.

## Clinical and copy vocabulary

- “Discouraging,” “empathetic,” and “empathy” appeared in spoken text or notes.
- “Sitting tolerance,” “guarding,” “traveling pattern,” and “evaluated” sounded clinical.
- “Magic question,” “make it real,” and “relive the win” reflected sales-training language.
- Decompression-specific meaning was often buried under generic persuasion copy.

## Punctuation

- `obj_send_info` used a colon to join two spoken thoughts.
- `lapsed_busy` used a semicolon to join spoken thoughts.
- `making_it_real` relied heavily on colons, nested quoted lines, and inline quoted reactions.
- Several titles and labels used inconsistent straight and curly quotation marks.

## Repeated identical objection tails

- `obj_came_back_stopped`, `obj_fixed_for_good`, `obj_injections_surgery`, `obj_managing_own`, `obj_think`, and `obj_send_info` shared the same long closing tail.
- The lapsed screens also repeated the same sequence of checks and recommendation lines.

## Chip and question mismatches

- Each `digging_in*` screen asked about treatment responses with no matching chip.
- The “Activities given up” chip did not mechanically match its question.
- Neck and back pain were combined in one question despite being separate chips.
- The old screens did not maintain a clean one-question-per-chip order.

## Chips

- No chip label or spoken phrase change is proposed.
