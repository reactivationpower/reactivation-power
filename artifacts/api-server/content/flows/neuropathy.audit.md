# Neuropathy flow audit

## Baseline

- Lint: 119 errors and 17 warnings.
- Word count: 4,256 total words.
- Breakdown: 3,885 title/content words and 371 choice-label words.
- Scope: 24 niche steps and 64 niche choices.

## Long multi-option questions

- `opening_question` put all three response options into one 40-word question.
- Each `digging_in*` screen stacked follow-up questions and examples.
- `making_it_real` repeatedly offered long lists of times, frequencies, and situations.
- `recommendation` asked the patient to accept an assumed effect on work and relationships.
- `bridge_no` asked three versions of why the patient would not come in.
- `obj_think` combined the question with cost, timing, and value options.

## Flourishes and intensifiers

- `transition*` used “obviously,” “absolutely,” and “completely up to date.”
- `resp_doing_great` called the result “hard-earned” and said the patient “earned this one.”
- `making_it_real` used “absolute worst day” and scripted dramatic reactions.
- Lapsed screens used “quietly,” “best reason there is,” and “gently share.”

## Sales stock phrases

- `recommendation` used “honored,” daily-help language, and time-or-money language.
- `making_it_real` used forced agreement language about symptoms not being okay.
- `lapsed_felt_fine` used “here’s the funny thing.”
- `lapsed_wait_pain` used the banned stay-ahead/behind line.
- Several screens opened with over-validation phrases such as “I completely understand.”

## Invented patient quotes and memories

- `resp_coming_back` and `resp_fully_back` claimed the caller remembered prior improvement.
- Both screens also assumed the patient remembered the same feeling.
- `lapsed_commitment` claimed a past goal and used “you told us.”
- `making_it_real` supplied fictional patient reactions and quoted answers.
- The rewrite points to notes where prior information is relevant.

## Token pile-ups

- `making_it_real`, `recommendation`, and all three niche objections used legacy `{{rec_*}}` tokens.
- `recommendation` repeated a legacy pronoun token throughout one paragraph.
- No screen needed repeated `{{provider_name}}`.
- The rewrite removes every `{{rec_pronoun}}`, `{{rec_pronoun_cap}}`, `{{rec_obj}}`, and `{{rec_poss}}`.

## Coaching directions

- `making_it_real` mixed spoken quotes into long coaching notes.
- It also glued parenthesized directions and sample reactions to spoken text.
- All five niche objections used an unparenthesized “Confirm and continue” direction.
- `lapsed_commitment` explained call strategy instead of giving a short action.

## Clinical and copy vocabulary

- `making_it_real` referenced Tylenol and made a duration-based care argument.
- `digging_in*` named gabapentin, Lyrica, Advil, supplements, and detailed symptom scenarios.
- `lapsed_felt_fine` asserted that nerves “heal quietly.”
- `lapsed_wait_pain` asserted that symptoms are usually the last sign of change.
- `bridge_no` used “evaluated” instead of “looked at” or “visit.”

## Punctuation and quoted lines

- `making_it_real` relied on colons, nested quotation marks, and quoted caller reactions.
- `lapsed_busy` joined thoughts with a semicolon.
- Objection tails used a colon to join an instruction and spoken questions.
- Several quoted passages contained multiple questions in one breath.

## Repeated objection and lapsed tails

- `obj_came_back_stopped`, `obj_frustrated`, `obj_bad_experience`, `obj_think`, and `obj_send_info` shared the same closing tail.
- That tail repeated two questions and an inline coaching note.
- Every lapsed response repeated the same sequence of checks, directions, and recommendation.
- The rewrite gives each niche objection a different closing check.
- Lapsed closing checks now vary by the reason given.

## Digging-in chip mismatches

- All three `digging_in*` screens asked seven body questions for six chips.
- The pain, stiffness, or discomfort question had no matching chip.
- The medications chip did not match the wording of the old medication question under lint’s stems.
- The old screens inserted long examples between chip questions.
- The rewrite asks six questions in chip order, then one recap question and the tap note.

## Chips

- The existing chips fit the rewritten questions in meaning and order.
- No chip label or spoken phrase change is proposed.
