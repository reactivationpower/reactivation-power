# Joint Pain flow audit

## Baseline

- Before word count: 4,097 total.
- Breakdown: 3,722 words in titles and content; 375 words in choice labels.
- Baseline lint: 128 errors and 11 warnings.

## Long multi-option questions

- `opening_question` combined the main question with three answer options.
- `resp_coming_back` and `resp_fully_back` stacked injury, life-change, and gradual-return questions.
- All three `digging_in*` screens repeatedly joined several questions in one breath.
- `making_it_real` stacked time ranges, frequency ranges, times of day, work, home, and hobbies.
- `obj_think` joined its question to three suggested answers.

## Flourishes and intensifiers

- `resp_doing_great` used “Joints that feel good are freedom” and “Plain and simple.”
- `transition*` used “obviously,” “absolutely,” and “completely up to date.”
- `digging_in*` used “joints talking,” “Tin Man,” and examples about bottles in the car.
- `making_it_real` used “everything was clicking” and “absolute worst day.”
- Several screens used “honestly,” “completely,” “exactly,” and “thrilled.”

## Sales stock phrases

- `recommendation` used “honored” and the time-or-money disclaimer.
- `making_it_real` used “exactly the kind of thing” and “every single day.”
- `lapsed_felt_fine` used “here’s the funny thing.”
- `lapsed_wait_pain` used “stay ahead of it instead of behind it.”
- Several objection screens used scripted “Confirm and continue” tails.

## Invented patient quotes or memories

- `resp_coming_back` and `resp_fully_back` claimed remembered relief.
- Those screens also assumed the patient remembered that feeling.
- `lapsed_commitment` claimed remembered goals and what the patient told the office.
- `obj_managing_own` claimed knowledge of how much better the patient felt.

## Token pile-ups

- `making_it_real`, `recommendation`, and niche objections used legacy `{{rec_*}}` tokens.
- `recommendation` repeated legacy pronoun tokens throughout one paragraph.
- No screen needed repeated `{{provider_name}}`; the rewrite uses it once where needed.

## Coaching directions

- `resp_coming_back` and `resp_fully_back` had unparenthesized coaching.
- `making_it_real` mixed directions, sample speech, and patient quotes inside notes.
- `making_it_real` also glued notes to spoken quoted lines.
- Objection tails mixed “Confirm and continue” directions with spoken questions.
- Lapsed screens carried long explanatory coaching notes.

## Clinical and copy vocabulary

- “Evaluation,” “non-judgmental,” “impacting,” and “productive” sounded formal.
- “Practice members,” “hold your progress,” and “real relief” sounded promotional.
- “Flare back up,” “slipping,” and “building underneath” made unsupported explanations.
- The rewrite keeps the joint-specific purpose in plain language.

## Punctuation

- `digging_in*` and `making_it_real` relied on colons and quoted sample lines.
- `lapsed_busy` used a semicolon to join thoughts.
- Several titles and labels used long colon constructions.
- Curly quoted dialogue appeared inside coaching notes and spoken paragraphs.

## Repeated tails

- Every niche `obj_*` screen ended with the same confirmation and concerns sequence.
- The rewrite gives each objection a different closing check.
- The lapsed screens also repeated the same multi-part close nearly word for word.

## Digging-in chip alignment

- All three `digging_in*` screens asked extra questions without matching chips.
- “Activities given up” appeared without a Joint Pain chip.
- The medication question did not use “medications” or “pills,” so lint missed it.
- The old questions combined several joints despite separate Knee, Hip, and Shoulder chips.
- The rewrite gives each chip one question, in chip order, followed by one pick question.

## Chips

- The existing chip list matches the rewritten questions.
- No chip label or spoken phrase change is proposed.
