# Patient Pipeline — landing-page sales video

## Locked brief

16:9 landscape for a website, separate from the walkthrough. Approximately 2:20–2:30, with final timing derived from natural narration rather than accelerated speech. Same narrator/voice settings as the demo walkthrough. Primary action: request a conversation. No landing-page changes, publishing, live calls, form submissions, or customer-data changes.

Show a virtual cursor for every depicted click and character-by-character entry for any depicted typing. Actual product captures, real response-to-screen transitions, privacy-safe seeded examples. No invented UI or fake successful submission. Do not add the rejected disclaimer overlay. The financial scenario is an incremental, one-month example above the office's current results. Per-patient value is an assumption, not guaranteed cash collected or profit. No annual framing.

## Director's treatment

Promise: two different conversations, one more consistent follow-up process.
Identity: existing Patient Pipeline logo; navy, warm ivory, teal and muted blue; existing walkthrough typography.
Hero: real caller viewport, not an isolated imitation script card.
Motion: two audience streams assemble into a product reveal; modest camera pushes focus real cursor actions; patient tokens combine into a revenue calculation.
Arc: recognize the opportunity, understand the two workflows, see repeatability, understand the financial example, request a conversation.
Payoff: quiet ivory brand lockup rather than another dashboard. Voice is continuous explanation; music remains subordinate.

## Shot 1 — opportunities

Purpose: identify both missed opportunities without blaming staff.
VO: “Your practice may already have opportunities that aren’t turning into appointments. Some are patients who haven’t been back in a while. Others are people who asked about care, but never booked. The challenge is giving your team a consistent way to follow up—and knowing what to say when someone answers.”
Exact editorial copy: “Opportunity is already here.” (0s); “Past patients” (4s); “New inquiries” (9s); “A consistent way to follow up” (16s).
Composition: navy field; large two-line headline in upper left, audience tracks right and below, 6% safe inset. Eight patient tokens are icons, not pictures of actual patients.
Choreography: staggered tokens at .4s intervals; past-patient rail resolves at 4s, inquiry rail at 9s; rails draw toward shared hub at 16s; camera pulls back in final 2s. No blank starting frame.
Handoff: shared hub becomes the logo reveal on ivory, with matched scale and a short split-field reveal.
Assets: verified existing logo, ordinary person/phone/calendar icons. Sound: continuous conversational voice and quiet instrumental, not a bullet reading.

## Shot 3 — reactivation

Purpose: demonstrate prior-service context and response-driven branching; staff make calls.
VO: “With reactivation, the conversation starts with the care that patient received before. As they explain what’s happening now, your team selects their response, and the script follows the conversation. Your staff still makes the call and listens to the patient—they’re simply not having to invent every next question.”
Exact editorial copy: “Reactivation”; “Care they received before”; “The conversation follows their answer”; original verified product screen text.
Composition: actual connected-call starting screen at large scale, showing the opening question and its three patient-state choices. Preserve the real button copy: “Still feeling great,” “It’s starting to flare up again,” and “Pretty much back to where I was.” These represent still doing good, starting to come back and back all the way. Top editorial strip outside the image; all cursor coordinates relative to that same screenshot. Small privacy masks cover individual names, phone numbers and email addresses without obscuring surrounding script text or labels.
Choreography: initial full-screen recognition; modest push around 5s; cursor moves to an actual patient-state response around 9s and clicks; its genuine next screen replaces the starting screen after the click; result readable; final pullback restores whole-screen context. Do not jump into an unexplained middle-of-script objection.
Handoff: viewport persists, teal rail changes to blue and lead viewport cross-replaces with a short camera settle.
Assets: real before/after captures and actual target coordinates. Sound: narration guides action timing. No synthetic caller audio.

## Shot 5 — repeatability

Purpose: show queue, outcome and note recording, not claim bookings occurred.
VO: “And it isn’t just about the script. You and your team have an organized way to see who needs attention, record what happened, and keep follow-up moving instead of relying on memory.”
Exact editorial copy: “Know who needs attention”; “Record what happened”; note text “Asked for a callback.”; original actual queue/outcome controls.
Composition: real queue shown as a tighter, list-focused crop with eight complete rows visible, rather than spending the frame on the dashboard header and two clipped rows; then the preserved actual logging view, with close framing only during text entry. Privacy masks cover individual names (including staff in call history), phone numbers and email addresses, not whole headers or script paragraphs. Any reconstructed text overlay is confined to the real existing note field.
Choreography: recognize queue; transition to logging view; cursor clicks the real notes field; “Asked for a callback.” types character by character over 2.5s. Do not click the disabled Log call control or depict a server success state. This deliberately stops at note entry rather than claiming a call was logged; the actual callback outcome opens a scheduling dialog, which is outside this short sales example.
Handoff: round avatar/queue marker becomes first of four additional reactivation tokens.
Assets: real sanitized queue and logging captures. Sound: same voice; no fake booking confirmation.

## Shot 6 — financial opportunity

At fifteen hundred dollars per patient, that’s ten thousand five hundred dollars in potential additional patient value from that month. That’s an illustration, not a forecast. Exact editorial copy: “What could one month of better follow-up mean?”; “4 additional reactivations”; “Above your current reactivation efforts”; “3 additional leads booked”; “Above your current lead bookings”; “7 additional patients”; “$1,500 value per patient”; “$10,500 potential additional patient value from one month”.
Composition: seven large patient tokens form two color-coded groups on navy; then combine into one band above the large total. No persistent disclaimer label, annual calculation, guaranteed monthly recurrence, profit claim, or financial calculator UI.
Choreography: four tokens reveal sequentially first; three next; each group's incremental baseline appears under its label; equation reveals at the matching spoken per-patient value assumption; large $10,500 resolves at spoken total; final hold preserves all assumptions.
Handoff: combined patient band tightens into a restrained brand rail and ivory field.
Assets: existing standard icons, SVG rail geometry; not a quantitative chart. Sound: voice states illustrative context naturally; music subtly lifts but stays quiet.
