# Reactivation Power — launch film

## Director's treatment
- **Ratio:** 16:9 landscape. A separately composed 9:16 companion uses the same narration and timeline.
- **Promise:** Your staff can have the right conversation with a former patient *and* with a new inquiry; both tools come together for one price during the launch.
- **Identity:** The existing product's ink/navy, off-white, and blue-teal accent; bold geometric display type with clean humanist body type. The real Patient Pipeline logo is used only on a high-contrast light field.
- **Hero:** Two illustrated, clearly simplified views of the product workflow, never a claim that the viewer is watching an unedited live session.
- **Motion:** A single contact card becomes a branching conversation; a campaign source becomes an inquiry card; the two workstreams meet as one offer.
- **Arc:** Quick problem hook → measured human conversation → quicker new-lead sequence → spacious product/offer resolution.
- **Payoff:** Both products resolve side by side into a single outlined price frame rather than repeating a UI view.

Timeline: 49 seconds total. The narration is paced naturally within each shot. Each shot has a live element on frame at entry; the outgoing card or line supplies a visual handoff rather than leaving an empty color field.

## Pre-code review
The five shots alternate graphic hook, cropped response panel, radial campaign card, editorial split and light brand lockup. Adjacent frames change field, scale and mechanism. Every scene has an internal content change after its entrance, plus a late handoff. The oversized quote, campaign origin, split workstreams and joined offer frame are distinct visual ideas. Text is short and readable without sound. No scene presents full scripts, real patient data, a fabricated price, guaranteed booking result or unedited footage claim. The desktop shot zones use a 6% margin; the vertical companion will place words and UI in separate stacked zones within its center-safe area. This is a sales film about staff-facing software, not medical treatment advice.
