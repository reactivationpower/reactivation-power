The user chose seven first-week calls (three in the first 24 hours, then days 2, 3, 5, 7), followed by days 10 and 14, then one check-in every two months if unanswered. A successful conversation ends that unanswered sequence; explicit callbacks take precedence.

**Why:** Balance prompt response to paid inquiries with less intrusive later follow-up. Do not silently return to the earlier ten-call first-week proposal.

**How to apply:** Keep the working queue distinct from all lead records. Missed slots must not become catch-up bursts. Calendar-selected appointments use confirmation scripts rather than the acquisition cadence.

Retain already-scheduled retries during rollout rather than retroactively replaying a new schedule.

**Why:** Existing promised callbacks and follow-up timing should not move earlier merely because a scheduling rule changed.

**How to apply:** Adopt the new cadence after the next logged outcome; intentionally parked unanswered leads may resume with one due check-in, not their entire missed history.

The user wants natural relative-date wording directly in the spoken script, with an exact date available as a reference.

**Why:** The timing and original service/offer help the person remember the inquiry without making the caller calculate dates. An import date is not evidence of when someone submitted a form.

**How to apply:** Preserve known original dates. Omit timing when unknown. Long-term follow-ups must not promise an expired offer is still available.
